## [AuK: Open-Source Foundational Model Unifies Speech Generation and Editing](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.08936) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers released AuK, an open-source foundational model that unifies speech generation and editing through a common interface of natural-language instructions and audio context, trained on roughly 3.03 billion instruction-audio instances and 1.95 million hours of effective supervision across five task families. The model combines a multimodal large language model for semantic conditioning, a VAE jointly trained on speech, general audio, and music, and a hybrid rectified-flow Transformer, with a distilled variant called AuK-Flash that performs 4-step inference and achieves a 4.5x wall-clock speedup over the full model. This is a significant open-source release for the speech and AI community because it unifies five task families—speech generation, content editing, enhancement and separation, paralinguistic editing, and acoustic editing—in a single foundational model, potentially reducing the need for separate specialized systems. Its scale and open weights could accelerate research and applications in instruction-controlled speech synthesis and editing, similar to how unified foundation models reshaped text and image generation. Training begins with generation-only warm-up followed by joint generation-editing pre-training, then uses human-feedback preference optimization for open-ended editing and reward-based reinforcement learning for speech generation. The architecture performs dual-stream MMDiT blocks followed by unified single-stream DiT blocks, and AuK-Flash uses consistency initialization and task-routed Decoupled DMD to enable 4-step inference without classifier-free guidance.

huggingface_papers · Hugging Face Papers · Sep 9, 00:00

**Background**: Speech generation and editing have traditionally been handled by separate models, each specialized for tasks like text-to-speech, noise removal, or style transfer. Recent advances in generative modeling, such as rectified flow and multimodal diffusion Transformers (MMDiT), have enabled high-quality synthesis by learning straight-line mappings between noise and data. A VAE (variational autoencoder) compresses audio into a compact latent space, and multimodal large language models provide semantic understanding of instructions. AuK builds on these ideas to create a single model that can follow natural-language instructions for both generating and editing speech.

## [Gander: End-to-End Omni Interaction Agent with Cerebellum-Brain Architecture](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.08977) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers released Gander, an end-to-end framework that unifies continuous multi-modal streaming, real-time full-duplex interaction, and agentic reasoning in a single model. It introduces a Cerebellum-Brain collaborative architecture and a chunk-level token stream design, with models, code, and data released to the community. Most current interactive AI systems are turn-based and half-duplex, so Gander's ability to handle interruptions, proactive feedback, and multi-party conversation could push real-time multimodal agents toward more natural human-like interaction. Its open release may accelerate research into full-duplex voice and video agents across the ecosystem. Gander separates real-time interaction (Cerebellum) from complex reasoning (Brain), with the two communicating through tool calling and an agent orchestration runtime; the Cerebellum uses a streaming Thinker-Talker architecture that flattens inputs and outputs into an ordered chunk-level token stream for low latency. Evaluations cover conversational ability, omni understanding, interactive capability, and agentic intelligence, and the model reportedly remains robust under background noise, multi-party interaction, and backchannel communication.

huggingface_papers · Hugging Face Papers · Sep 9, 00:00

**Background**: Full-duplex interaction means both sides can send and receive information simultaneously, as in a phone call, whereas earlier voice AI systems were half-duplex: the user speaks, the system processes, then responds. The Cerebellum-Brain naming draws on neuroscience, where the cerebellum handles fast, split-second reactions while the brain handles deliberate reasoning. Chunk-level token streaming is a technique for processing continuous audio and video in small ordered units rather than waiting for complete turns.

## [Qwen 3.8 Reasoning Traces Mirror GPT-5.5 Pro, Suggesting Distillation](https://gist.github.com/wsxiaoys/e0286dc6bb624ff5fdf49e7f4c528ba3) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A GitHub gist by wsxiaoys demonstrates that Qwen 3.8's reasoning prefills correlate strongly with those of GPT-5.5 Pro, using a method that recovers readable chain-of-thought from closed models and feeds the first 1% of it as a prefill to the open-source model. The finding, discussed on Hacker News with 138 points and 58 comments, suggests Qwen 3.8 may have been trained on GPT-5.5 Pro's reasoning traces. If confirmed, this would be one of the clearest public cases of reasoning-trace distillation from a frontier closed model into a leading open-weight model, raising questions about training data provenance, model licensing, and how the AI community evaluates claims of independent capability. It also highlights a new forensic technique for detecting distillation that could become standard practice for auditing model lineage. The method relies on the 'stolen thoughts' exploit, which recovers readable chain-of-thought from OpenAI and Anthropic models; the authors run a benchmark with a state-of-the-art model, extract the first 1% of its CoT, and use it as a prefill for the open-source model to see if it continues in the same style. Caveats include that correlation does not prove how much training data came from GPT-5.5 Pro, and that stylistic influence may not equate to genuine capability transfer.

hackernews · wsxiaoys · Sep 9, 17:24 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49630026)

**Background**: Model distillation is the practice of training a student model using signals from a teacher model, such as probabilities, outputs, or reasoning traces. Reasoning distillation specifically uses serialized chain-of-thought traces from a stronger model to supervise a smaller or open model, a technique shown to add new reasoning primitives beyond what reinforcement learning alone provides. The 'stolen thoughts' paper demonstrated that hidden reasoning traces from closed models can be partially recovered and used to detect such distillation.
